# Design Decisions

## Submission Experience

The form was intentionally designed to balance two competing priorities:

1. Collect enough information for meaningful proposal evaluation.
2. Keep the submission process concise enough to encourage completion.

Rather than requesting extensive presentation materials during the proposal stage, only the information required for an initial review is collected.

---

## Presenter Information

Basic presenter information is collected first to establish context before requesting presentation details.

Optional fields such as LinkedIn profiles and degree programs provide additional context without creating barriers for submission.

---

## Presentation Experience

Presentation experience is collected using a multiple-choice question followed by an optional description of previous presentations.

This allows experienced presenters to provide additional information while ensuring first-time presenters are not discouraged from applying.

---

## Presentation Proposal

Presentation descriptions were limited in length to encourage concise, focused proposals.

Participant takeaways replace formal learning objectives to encourage more natural responses while still communicating expected outcomes.

---

## User Experience

Content is grouped into logical sections using headings and visual dividers.

This improves readability and makes the proposal process feel shorter despite collecting multiple pieces of information.

---

## Accessibility

Clear instructions and descriptive help text were included throughout the form to reduce ambiguity and improve completion rates.
